Every money row is written `status = 'completed'` with `event_id` set when it belongs to an event.

- **Idempotency keys** (unique partial index on `transactions.idempotency_key`, so inserts must use `ON CONFLICT (idempotency_key) WHERE idempotency_key IS NOT NULL DO NOTHING`): `bet:<holdId>`, `release:<eventId>:<userId>:<seq>`, `payout:<eventId>:<userId>:<seq>`, `refund:<eventId>:<holdId>:<seq>`, `reverse:<originalTxId>`, `stripe:<stripeEventId>`, `withdraw:<userId>:<clientKey>`. `<seq>` is a settlement generation counter — without it a resolve → unresolve → re-resolve cycle silently pays nobody and strands the pot.
- **API contract for siblings:** `POST /api/events` accepts `payment_type`, `stake_amount` (null = each bettor picks their own), and passes `subject_user_id` / `notify_subject` straight through (n4's fields — I made the route not drop them so n4 does not have to touch my file). `GET /api/events?id=` gained `escrow_total`. `GET /api/wallet?userId=&eventId=` gained `escrow_held_total`, `available` and an `event` block; `wallet` and `transactions` stay at the top level. `POST /api/events/resolve` accepts `{ event_id, action: 'cancel' }` to refund every stake. Money errors return `{ error, code, ...details }` — clients should branch on `code` (`INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS` carries `balance`/`required`/`shortfall`), never on the message.
- **A cancelled event is `status = 'resolved'` with `winning_side IS NULL`** (no magic string, no new enum value, so `lib/types.ts` did not need changing). The web client keys its "cancelled" banner off exactly that.
- **Deliberate behaviour changes:** cash events reject late bets (escrowed money would be stranded, since `calculateNetResults` ignores late bets); bets on cash events cannot be deleted (`CASH_BET_IMMUTABLE`) — cancel the event to refund instead; `app/api/events/delete` was unauthenticated and now requires auth + the resolver/admin rule, and refuses to delete a cash event with outstanding escrow (the `escrow_holds` CASCADE would have destroyed real money).

## n4: Subject privacy, one central non-bypassable notification filter, real per-user preferences
- **The suppression rule (this is the contract other nodes must honour).** An event may name a group member as its SUBJECT (`events.subject_user_id`) and the creator may turn `events.notify_subject` off. When `notify_subject = false`:
  - The subject receives **no push at all** about that event — not its creation, not bets placed on it, not comments, not its resolution or cancellation. This holds **forever**, including after the event resolves. Resolution does not "release" the notification; being told about it later by a ping is exactly the surprise the creator opted out of.
  - The event **stays out of the subject's activity feed while it is unresolved**, and **appears there normally once `status = 'resolved'`**. Hiding it forever would make the group's shared ledger lie about its own history, and the outcome is public at that point.
  - **Direct links still work.** This is notification privacy, not access control: the subject is a group member and can open `/events/<id>` or browse the group and see everything. The create form says this in plain words — the UI never promises secrecy the app cannot keep.
